### Decode at large batch

The model is within ~1% wherever it was fitted, but it underpredicts decode at batch ≥ 32:
by 40% at batch 160. Measured ITL grows almost linearly with batch
from 16 to 256: 11.51 ms + 97 µs per sequence (r² 0.986). The roofline instead stays
flat on the weight read until compute crosses it at batch 192. Two readings, both outside schema v1:

- Small-M GEMMs run well below prefill efficiency. Implied decode η_c = FLOPs ÷ ((ITL − t_o) × peak),
  shown where decode could be compute-bound (batch ≥ 128):

  | Batch | Implied decode η_c |
  |---|---|
  | 128 | 0.307 |
  | 160 | 0.299 |
  | 192 | 0.342 |
  | 224 | 0.347 |
  | 256 | 0.375 |

- Or a per-sequence cost (sampling, scheduler and input prep, KV gather) of about 97 µs per sequence per step,
  which the roofline has no term for.

Consequence: at high batch the simulator's replica is faster than the real one, so the simulated
saturation knee (tab 2) lands at a higher rate than R3's. B4 should compare them. A per-sequence
step term would be a schema change for the integrator.

Cold TTFT matches the model (it is R1's fit). Warm TTFT does not: the excess over the model
grows with the cached prefix at **5.9 µs per cached token** (r² 0.995), which is
192 ms at a 32,768-token prefix. The step model charges a cache hit nothing
beyond the suffix's attention to it. Likely sources: the API server tokenizing the whole prompt, and
the scheduler hashing and looking up its blocks. The lesson (a cache hit is far cheaper) holds, since
warm is 5–40% of cold, but simulated warm TTFT is optimistic at long prefixes. Candidate term for B4:
a per-prompt-token cost outside the GPU step.
