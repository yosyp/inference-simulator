### Decode at large batch

B3's model was within ~1% wherever it was fitted, but it underpredicted decode at batch ≥ 32:
by 40% at batch 160. Measured ITL grows almost linearly with batch
from 16 to 256: 11.51 ms + 97 µs per sequence (r² 0.986). The roofline instead stays
flat on the weight read until compute crosses it at batch 192. Two readings:

- Small-M GEMMs run well below prefill efficiency. Implied decode η_c = FLOPs ÷ ((ITL − t_o) × peak),
  shown where decode could be compute-bound (batch ≥ 128):

  | Batch | Implied decode η_c |
  |---|---|
  | 128 | 0.307 |
  | 160 | 0.299 |
  | 192 | 0.342 |
  | 224 | 0.347 |
  | 256 | 0.375 |

- Or a per-sequence cost (sampling, scheduler and input prep, KV gather) of about 97 µs per sequence per step,
  which the roofline has no term for.

X4a takes the second, with one parameter: `decodePerSeqMs` = 52.0 µs. It is smaller than the
raw 97 µs slope because the roofline already charges the growing KV read and, from batch
192, compute. The worst X4a residual at batch ≥ 16 is -11.3% at batch 160
(B3: -39.9%).

Cold TTFT matches the model (it is R1's fit). Warm TTFT did not under B3: the excess over the model
grows with the cached prefix at 5.9 µs per cached token (r² 0.995), which is
192 ms at a 32,768-token prefix. The step model charged a cache hit nothing
beyond the suffix's attention to it. Likely sources: the API server tokenizing the whole prompt, and
the scheduler hashing and looking up its blocks. The lesson (a cache hit is far cheaper) holds, since
warm is 5–40% of cold.

X4a: `cachedTokenMs` = **6.20 µs** per cached token, least squares through the origin of the warm
excess over the X4a model, charged to the step that admits the request. A per-prompt-token cost is the
other reading (the whole prompt is tokenized); charging only hits leaves cold TTFT, R1's fit, unchanged.
